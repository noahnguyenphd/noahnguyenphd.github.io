Research Interests

  • Cybersecurity and Information Assurance

Research Stream

My research stream is cybersecurity in organizations. I study employees' protective and risky security behaviors as work moves from traditional IT to AI and agentic AI systems. Protective behaviors include complying with a security control, asking for access or an exception, and reporting. Risky behaviors include violating a control, working around it, using shadow IT and AI, and hiding the behavior.

The overarching question is: What are the drivers and consequences of employees' protective and risky security behaviors? Three research questions follow from it.

  • RQ1 (Drivers): What drives employees' protective and risky security behaviors? This question includes shadow AI and the sharing of sensitive data with AI tools.
  • RQ2 (Interventions): How can employees' protective security behaviors be enhanced and risky ones reduced? This question includes the security governance of AI and AI agents.
  • RQ3 (Consequences and broader issues): What are the consequences of employees' protective and risky security behaviors, and what broader issues follow for information assurance, resilience, and security leadership?

I also work on an alternative stream outside these three research questions. It covers generative AI, misinformation, and AI governance.

Research Methods

I use behavioral, econometric, and computational methods:

  • Survey Research: Cross-sectional, longitudinal, and multi-wave surveys of employees and organizations.
  • Experiments: Vignette and field experiments that test theoretical mechanisms and support causal inference.
  • Interviews: Interviews that establish a phenomenon before experiments test it.
  • Field and Archival Data: Econometric analysis of archival data, incident and downtime records, and log data.
  • Statistical Modeling: Structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM and CB-SEM) and regression techniques.
  • Computational Methods: Machine learning, natural language processing (NLP), large language models (LLMs), data mining, and web scraping.
  • Systematic Reviews: Systematic literature reviews, meta-analysis, and bibliometric analysis.

Effects of Cybersecurity Readiness on Firm Performance: Evidence from Conference Calls

Status: Finalizing for submission

Collaboration: Florida Atlantic University, Binghamton University, University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa

Machine learning and econometric analysis of how a firm's cybersecurity readiness relates to data breaches and firm performance.

Clinical Information Systems Under Natural Disasters: Dual Mechanisms of Staff Competence and System Capacity in Hospital Response and Recovery

Status: Under review

Collaboration: University of Texas at San Antonio, Binghamton University, Dakota State University, Oregon State University

A dual-mechanism framework that separates staff competence from system capacity in hospital disaster response and recovery. We test it with survey data from hospital employees in hurricane-affected regions.
